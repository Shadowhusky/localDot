# Evidence-led research

Turn the task into answerable questions and choose the smallest adequate source set. For an ordinary fact, a current authoritative source may suffice; a disputed or consequential claim needs independent corroboration. Search primary docs, datasets, standards and original reports before summaries. Publication date and event date are different.

Keep a compact evidence table in the task folder when the investigation is substantial: claim, source URL/title/date, relevant finding, confidence and unresolved contradiction. Distinguish source facts from your inference. Follow links to original evidence; do not repeat a search snippet as a verified finding. Check denominators, population, time range and missing data before comparing statistics.

Read only relevant sections, avoid fetching the same source repeatedly, and stop when the question is resolved or further evidence is unlikely to change the answer within budget. Use data-analysis for computation and report-writing for the final report. Use a specialist skill discovered from a maintained primary source when the domain needs methods you do not have. Preserve citations through summarization; deliver the answer and evidence, not merely a research plan. If a child researcher is still running, wait for its result or clearly mark the work incomplete.
