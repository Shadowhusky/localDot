# Replay acceptance — October 5, 2026

The available host was a Mac Studio (`Mac15,14`, Apple M3 Ultra). These results are local
acceptance measurements, not a claim about M1 laptop performance. The optional 60-second M1
laptop comparison of no viewer / one viewer / one viewer plus recorder was not performed.

## Recorded desktop

The real-cell fixture ran the bundled multi-app puppet at 2× with no browser viewer connected.
All ten GUI steps completed; recording was finalized as AVC 2560×1600 with no recording gaps.
Pointer timings matched the Fast formula and the cell reported zero relay viewers. The eight
steps that capture screenshots were compared at their observe timestamps; the two explicit
wait steps do not create new screenshots.

Each presented Replay video frame was sampled as a 320×200 RGBA image and compared with the
corresponding saved thumbnail. Mean absolute RGB differences ranged from **1.886 to 6.162**
on a 0–255 scale, all below the acceptance bound of 12. The separate running-session case
switched from Replay to Watch and received live frames within ten seconds, then confirmed Stop.

Export of the recorded desktop at 4× with idle compression produced a 1920×1200 AVC MP4:

All four recorded-session browser checks passed in 65.464 seconds, including setup and owned
resource cleanup. The evidence files are `recorded-session-evidence.json`,
`recorded-frame-differences.json`, `recorded-export.json`, and `recorded-replay.png` under
`apps/web/e2e/out/`.

## Long synthetic export

The separate two-hour synthetic recording was exported at 8× with idle compression. It
produced 26,190 frames in 154.739 seconds (169.253 encoded frames per second), a 12.425079 MB
file, and 1,949,980 bytes of additional main-thread heap after garbage collection. Peak live
video frames were three and the actual native encoder queue peaked at four. Main-thread heap
does not measure the export worker's entire memory use.

Five export browser cases passed: playable MP4, floating sheet and cancellation, privacy and
takeover holds, matching full-frame hashes across two separate loads, and bounded long-session
export. A separate screenshot check verified the footer within a 375×480 viewport after the
opening animation completed. Chrome playback and ffprobe passed; QuickTime playback remains
unverified because the native-app check encountered a locked Mac.

## Verification limits

The final host suite passed 3,232 tests in 338 files with `pnpm test --maxWorkers=4`; types and
lint passed. The first unrestricted-concurrency host run timed out in an existing 257-session
history test while browser acceptance was active. The four-worker rerun preserved its original
five-second timeout and assertions. Existing lint warnings remain in retained review fixtures.

The first recorded-session attempt incorrectly expected an observe for wait steps. The second
used only recorder activity when computing export duration, omitting event activity. Both
fixtures were corrected: every screenshot-producing step is checked, and duration is compared
against the actual Replay timeline. These failed attempts remain in the controller's evidence.
Neither required a production behavior change. The added thumbnail probe remains opt-in.

Task15's independent LIGHT review passed with no findings and forty scoped checks, including
seven additional worker/file lifecycle cases. Its two regression files were retained unchanged;
all seven added cases and the four thumbnail-probe cases pass together in the Task16 source.

The first broader Docker run passed eleven suites but failed the standalone desktop-image
setup because Docker reported no published port 3000. All nineteen desktop-image checks passed
on an isolated rerun without changing production code or assertions. The full retry then passed
78 checks in 12 suites; 14 checks in six explicitly gated suites were skipped. Initial failures
remain in the evidence and are not counted as passing runs.

The full browser run passed 50 cases and skipped two. Four failures were resolved in scoped

Task16's single LIGHT review found two Important coverage/fixture issues and one Minor guide

## Recorder with two viewers

Two separate cases retain the original two-viewer experiment and add a recording session.
Both animate a full 2560×1600 noise image and freeze one viewer for six five-second windows,
requiring a real socket backlog over 128 KiB while the other viewer exceeds half its measured
pre-freeze rate. The original case retains its greater-than-10-fps recovery check. The new
recording case checks recovery against the same recording workload's measured rate, and also
requires committed fragments and bytes to increase in every freeze window. This is a no-stall
experiment under extreme screen noise, not a normal-desktop performance benchmark.

Both cases pass. The original viewer recovered at 14.5 fps. With recording active, the healthy
viewer's six windows measured 8.6–10.0 fps against an 8.4-fps pre-freeze sample; the resumed
viewer reached 11 fps. The largest socket backlog was 4,190,912 bytes. Recording advanced from
2 fragments before the freeze to 78 during it and 84 at completion, with no gaps. The UI Stop
ended the session as stopped before the test stopped and removed its computer. Both cases
together took 126.553 seconds, with each owning its own daemon/keyd home and cell.

Retained scoped failures include a sandbox socket-binding denial, one low-frame-rate startup,
one Chromium launch failure after a container pause, and an initial cleanup that waited for
the idle computer button during an active task. The latter was corrected to use task Stop
first. An intermediate recovery assertion compared the recording workload to the older fixed
10-fps threshold; separating the original case from the new workload preserves the original
check and gives the recording case a like-for-like rate comparison. QuickTime playback remains
unverified because the native-app attempt encountered a locked Mac. M6 is not fully accepted
until that playback check is completed.
